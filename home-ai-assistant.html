Home JARVIS (Home AI Assistant)

A voice assistant for my own home that keeps as much as possible on my own hardware. It runs Home Assistant OS in a virtual machine on a Windows PC I already own. The aim is to keep recurring costs to a minimum: Google charges a one-off US$5 fee for Device Access (needed for the Nest connection), and optional Claude answers are billed per use, only after I say yes.

Google Nest connection

Home JARVIS is the name of my private home-automation app. It connects my own Google Nest devices (a doorbell, a porch camera and a thermostat) to my own Home Assistant system through Google's Smart Device Management API. Day to day I control the thermostat through a local connection instead. It is not a public service, has no sign-ups or other users, and touches only my own devices.

Home JARVIS privacy policy

What stays local, what uses the cloud

  • Local: speech recognition and spoken replies, a free built-in command matcher that is tried before Claude, and control of devices on my home network. A separate webcam relay on the PC serves a video stream (RTSP) only to the home server.
  • Cloud: Nest doorbell and porch-camera events and live video come through Google's Smart Device Management API, and the cameras stream by WebRTC. Optional Claude answers go to Anthropic, only after a spoken yes.

What it does

  • Speech in and out runs locally.
  • Planned: a hands-free "Hey Jarvis" wake word, which needs a room microphone I have not added yet.
  • Conversation is free and local first. Paid Claude answers happen only after a spoken yes within 60 seconds. A live test billed exactly one call.
  • Doorbell rings arrive through Google's Smart Device Management API as one-tap phone alerts that open the live view, and the porch camera's motion events are received too.
  • A pet dashboard with three buttons, each asking for confirmation first. TV time turns the TV on with a video I picked and switches it off after a set time. Calm audio plays a calm radio station on a smart speaker for a set time. Stop all ends both. Scheduled versions are built but stay off until I choose times.

How I built it

  • Safe defaults. New devices are not exposed to voice by default, and physical actions sit behind a confirmation.
  • Careful live changes. Every live change starts with a full backup, then is validated and read back. API calls are logged with secrets masked, versions and logs are re-read afterwards, and the evidence is scanned for hardware addresses, device identifiers and tokens before it is kept.
  • Stale-data rule. A reading shows its age first, and is flagged if the device was disconnected or the data is over 6 hours old.
